# EgoHand-Bench — Final Report

**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Hardware:** NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5060 Laptop GPU, 8,151 MiB total, Blackwell `sm_120`  
**Effective target:** ≤6,000 MB peak process/driver VRAM (leaving laptop compositor headroom)

## 1. Summary

This project compared a cheap CPU keypoint floor (MediaPipe), lightweight MANO mesh reconstruction
(MobRecon), and WiLoR default/fast on a common 200-image seeded FreiHAND dev subset. **WiLoR-fast
is the measured winner** under the stated weights: 33.51 ms mean reconstruction pipeline latency,
39.42 ms p95, 29.84 FPS, 3,014 MB nvidia-smi peak, and 5.901 mm PA-MPJPE. It retains a MANO mesh,
uses the official authors' FP16/depth-pruning fast mode, and remains comfortably inside the 6GB
ceiling. MediaPipe is almost as fast on CPU but has 15.883 mm PA-MPJPE and no mesh; MobRecon is
fast and small but its measured accuracy is provisional because its legacy OpenMesh spiral ordering
could not be reproduced exactly without its obsolete native build dependencies.

## 3. Quantitative model comparison

Source: `results/raw/*.json`, generated by `benchmark/run_benchmark.py` with 20 warmups and 200
CUDA-event (GPU) or wall-clock (CPU) timed iterations.

| Model | Variant | Mean ms | p95 ms | FPS | PA-MPJPE mm | PA-MPVPE mm | F@5mm | F@15mm | nvidia-smi peak MB | Predictions / misses |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| MediaPipe | default (CPU) | 34.351 | 46.503 | 29.11 | 15.883 ± 6.270 | — | — | — | — | 180 / 20 |
| MobRecon | DenseStack | 22.710 | 33.778 | 44.03 | 24.774 ± 6.682* | 29.176 | 0.258 | 0.737 | 1,292 | 200 / 0 |
| WiLoR | default | 69.531 | 77.272 | 14.38 | 5.742 ± 2.605 | 5.338 | (metric corrected after this run) | — | 4,235 | 200 / 0 |
| WiLoR | **fast** | **33.508** | **39.424** | **29.84** | **5.901 ± 2.590** | **5.455** | **0.800** | **0.990** | **3,014** | 200 / 0 |

`*` MobRecon accuracy is **provisional**, not a paper-quality result: its checkpoint expects the
upstream OpenMesh spiral neighbor ordering. The environment lacked a binary OpenMesh wheel and a
compiler; the tested fallback uses deterministic BFS topology, so the forward/latency/VRAM row is
real but its accuracy should not drive a final model claim.

The WiLoR default F-score row was produced before the metric-unit/alignment bug was corrected;
WiLoR-fast and later rows use the corrected aligned metric implementation. PA-MPJPE/PA-MPVPE are
valid for both WiLoR rows because those functions were independent of the F-score bug.

## 4. Decision

Source: `results/decision.md` and `results/comparison_table.md`. Default weights were retained:
latency 0.35, PA-MPJPE 0.35, peak VRAM 0.20, deployment complexity 0.10.

| Rank | Candidate | Final score |
|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | **WiLoR-fast** | **0.7493** |
| 2 | MobRecon | 0.6000 |
| 3 | MediaPipe | 0.5265 |
| 4 | WiLoR-default | 0.4000 |

**Winner: WiLoR-fast.** It gives the best balanced score and the needed MANO mesh while staying
under budget. **Runner-up: MobRecon**, which would be more attractive if its exact official spiral
ordering were restored and its accuracy returned near the published 6.9mm range; its current
provisional 24.77mm result is not sufficient to displace WiLoR-fast.

The inductive-bias priors were partly validated. The decoupled detector/reconstruction design
helps the WiLoR fast path remain practical, and the authors' built-in fast mode produced about
2.07× lower measured latency than WiLoR default (69.53→33.51ms) for only +0.159mm PA-MPJPE. MANO
outputs were valuable: WiLoR-fast has both a low-dimensional parameter output and a dense mesh,
whereas MediaPipe's keypoints are cheaper but materially less accurate and not directly
retargetable. The cheap-floor prior also held: MediaPipe is competitive on speed but does not
match the mesh-capable model's accuracy. MobRecon's speed/size prior held, but its legacy runtime
fragility and unverified spiral-order reproduction undermined its accuracy result.

## 6. Optimization results

This stage went from a documented ONNX-export blocker to a verified native TensorRT FP16 engine.
Every number below is measured on the exported WiLoR-fast reconstruction graph (detector/crop
outside the graph, the correct deployment split). Source: `optimize/*.py`, `results/raw/ort-latency.json`,
`results/raw/trt-latency.json`, `results/raw/trt-accuracy-gate.json`, `results/raw/latency-breakdown.json`.

### 6.1 ONNX export + numeric validation — RESOLVED

Earlier `torch.onnx.export(dynamo=True)` failed in the MANO parent-joint Python loop and TorchScript
tracing hit Lightning's `trainer` introspection. The blocker was resolved by:

- installing/patching the official WiLoR dependency tree (`smplx`, `chumpy` patched for numpy>=2,
  `pytorch_lightning`, `yacs`, `pyrender`, `timm`, `scikit-image`) into the `egohand` env;
- tracing a **plain function** around `forward_step` (not the LightningModule) so `trainer`
  introspection is bypassed;
- unwrapping `torch.compile` (`_orig_mod`) to eager for trace, and freezing `requires_grad_`
  so traced weights are not captured as grad constants.

Exported graph: `results/onnx/wilor-fast.onnx` (+ 1.2 GB external FP16 constants). Inputs:
`image [1,3,256,256] float16` (the `right` scalar baked in as a trace constant for the
single-hand graph). Numeric validation against native PyTorch (`optimize/validate_onnx.py`):

| Backend | joints max|Δ| | verts max|Δ| | PA-MPJPE vs PyTorch |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| ORT CPU EP | 2.25e-3 | 2.52e-3 | 0.656 mm |
| ORT CUDA EP | 2.20e-3 | 2.46e-3 | 0.662 mm |

Residuals are FP16 round-off; the graph is numerically faithful.

### 6.2 Backend latency benchmark — native PyTorch wins over ORT

`optimize/bench_onnx_latency.py`, 100 timed iterations after warmup, CUDA events for GPU paths:

| Path | Mean ms | p50 ms | p95 ms | p99 ms | FPS |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Native PyTorch (FP16) | 24.012 | 23.008 | 31.550 | 39.330 | 41.65 |
| ORT CUDA EP | 52.110 | 49.287 | 84.302 | 91.197 | 19.19 |
| ORT CPU EP | 1209.492 | 1184.644 | 1599.495 | 1692.121 | 0.83 |

ORT's CUDA EP is **2.2× slower** than native PyTorch on this FP16 ViT+MANO graph. The ORT TensorRT
EP could not load: ORT 1.23.2 links `libnvinfer.so.10` (TRT 10.x) while the env's TRT 11.2 ships
`libnvinfer.so.11`. Documented as an ORT-TRT-EP version mismatch; native TRT below is the
engineering-correct path.

### 6.3 Native TensorRT FP16 engine — ADOPTED

`optimize/build_trt_engine.py` builds a native FP16 `.plan` via TRT Python bindings. The env's
TRT 11.2 is a CUDA-13 build (`libnvrtc.so.13`) incompatible with the CUDA-12.8 nvidia runtime
libs (`cudaError 35: driver version insufficient`); resolved by installing `tensorrt-cu12==10.16`
(`libnvinfer.so.10`, sm_120 builder resources present). Engine: `results/trt/wilor-fast-fp16.plan`.

`optimize/bench_trt.py`, 200 CUDA-event iterations:

| Path | Mean ms | p50 ms | p95 ms | p99 ms | FPS |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **TRT FP16 engine** | **13.717** | **13.605** | **18.759** | **20.907** | **72.90** |
| Native PyTorch (ref) | 24.012 | 23.008 | 31.550 | 39.330 | 41.65 |

**1.75× faster than native, 3.8× faster than ORT-CUDA.** Accuracy gate vs PyTorch:
joints max|Δ|=2.12e-3, verts max|Δ|=2.40e-3, PA-MPJPE-vs-PyTorch = 0.667 mm → **PASS**.

### 6.4 Accuracy gate vs GT (FP16 vs INT8)

`optimize/bench_trt_gate.py`, 100 dev crops, PA-MPJPE vs ground truth:

| Engine | PA-MPJPE vs GT | Decision |
|---|---:|---|
| TRT FP16 | 5.544 ± 2.137 mm | baseline (matches PyTorch 5.9mm) |
| TRT INT8 (entropy PTQ, 64 crops) | 9.438 ± 2.686 mm | **REJECT** (Δ=+3.89mm vs FP16, gate +0.5mm) |

INT8 PTQ (`optimize/build_trt_int8.py`, `IInt8EntropyCalibrator2`) reaches 10.30 ms / 97 FPS
but **badly degrades accuracy**: the MANO LBS parent-joint chain amplifies INT8 quantization
error. This is an honest negative result — INT8 is not adopted; FP16 is the deployed precision.

### 6.5 End-to-end latency breakdown

`optimize/bench_breakdown.py` (separate stages, 50 iterations, CUDA events for GPU stages):

| Stage | Mean ms | p95 ms |
|---|---:|---:|
| Preprocess + YOLO detector (CPU+GPU) | 17.487 | 21.303 |
| Crop/collate (ViTDetDataset) | 2.875 | 5.619 |
| Reconstruction forward (eager FP16) | 27.661 | 34.042 |
| Postprocess (tensor→numpy) | 0.312 | 0.770 |
| Sum of stages | 48.335 | — |

The detector+preprocess dominates wall-clock (17.5ms). Swapping eager forward (27.7ms) for the
TRT FP16 engine (13.7ms) cuts the reconstruction stage by ~50%; the remaining end-to-end
bottleneck is the CPU/GPU detector path, which is the next optimization target (fused preprocessing,
pinned memory, or a lighter detector).

### 6.6 Repeated WiLoR-fast measurements (variance)

Three fresh processes, 200 CUDA-event iterations each (`benchmark/run_benchmark.py`):

| Run | Mean ms | p95 ms | PA-MPJPE mm |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 41.61 | 50.63 | 5.903 |
| 2 | 33.71 | 41.42 | 5.903 |
| 3 | 35.62 | 41.84 | 5.903 |

Accuracy is bit-stable (5.903mm across all runs). Wall-clock latency varies ±4ms across
processes (background GPU contention from the laptop compositor); the TRT engine's lower
p95 (18.8ms vs 41ms) is the practical stability win.

### 6.7 MobRecon spiral ordering — still provisional

An attempt to replicate OpenMesh's half-edge-ordered one-ring traversal (`extract_spirals`)
in pure Python was tried but **did not match OpenMesh's exact winding** (accuracy worsened to
35.7mm vs the BFS fallback's 24.8mm, both far from the published 6.9mm). OpenMesh has no
buildable wheel for py3.10 in this env (conda-forge tops out at py3.8, no compiler for a source
build). MobRecon stays **provisional** — latency/VRAM are real, accuracy is not publishable until
OpenMesh is restored or the released precomputed spiral cache is reconciled.

## 7. Final smoke test

Command:

```bash
export WILOR_ROOT=$HOME/src/WiLoR
export TORCH_FORCE_NO_WEIGHTS_ONLY_LOAD=1
python tests/smoke_test.py --max-frames 150
```

Measured result:

- 5/5 FreiHAND smoke images: joints shape `(21,3)` — **PASS**
- Synthetic clip: 150 frames, 149 detections / 1 detector miss — **PASS** (misses are valid
  detector behavior under synthetic occlusion; no crash)
- End-to-end wall-clock mean: **42.54ms**, p95 **49.05ms** — **latency target <33.33ms: FAIL**
  because this includes detector and Python preprocessing; reconstruction-only CUDA-event result
  is 33.51ms mean / 39.42ms p95
- Torch peak allocated: **1,484.76MB** — **PASS** against 6,000MB
- Memory range during clip: **0.001MB** — **PASS**, no growing-latency/memory leak signal
- `smoke test: PASS` for crash, shape, VRAM, and leak assertions.

The end-to-end 30FPS target is not met by the current official Python detector+preprocessing path;
that gap is real and should be addressed by fusing/caching preprocessing and moving detector/crop
into an optimized runtime if strict 30FPS whole-frame operation is required.

## 8. HaMeR status

HaMeR is **environment-blocked**, not skipped. The official `hamer_demo_data.tar.gz` (1.8 GB) was
fully extracted (`_DATA/vitpose_ckpts/vitpose+_huge/wholebody.pth`, `MANO_RIGHT.pkl`) and the
`hamer` package installed editable into its env. The hard blocker is **detectron2**: HaMeR's
`demo.py` requires the ViTDet/RegNetY detector via detectron2, which has **no prebuilt wheel for
PyTorch 2.11 / `sm_120`** and must be compiled from source. The env has no `nvcc` (CUDA toolkit
not installed) and `pip install detectron2` finds no matching distribution, so the CUDA ops
cannot be built. No fake or estimated HaMeR row was created. HaMeR can be unblocked by installing
the CUDA toolkit + a detectron2 source build, or by swapping in an external detector that does
not depend on detectron2.

## 9. Limitations and next work

- Single dominant hand protocol; no two-hand interactions.
- PA-MPJPE cross-convention comparison is aligned; raw MPJPE is not primary.
- FreiHAND is research/non-commercial; WiLoR weights are CC-BY-NC-ND; MANO has its own license.
- MobRecon accuracy stays provisional until OpenMesh's exact spiral ordering is restored.
- HaMeR is blocked on detectron2 compilation (no nvcc / sm_120 wheel).
- INT8 PTQ was rejected by the accuracy gate (+3.89mm vs FP16). FP16 is the deployed precision.
- The TRT FP16 engine covers the reconstruction graph only; detector/crop stay outside. The
  next end-to-end win is fusing preprocessing + a lighter detector to cut the 17.5ms
  detector stage (the new dominant bottleneck once reconstruction is 13.7ms).
- Stage 10 (CUDA Graphs/Triton) was **not attempted**; with a static TRT graph already at
  13.7ms/72.9FPS and the detector now the bottleneck, CUDA Graphs would need to wrap the
  detector+crop path, not the reconstruction graph.
